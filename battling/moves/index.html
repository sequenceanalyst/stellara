Pokémon on Stellara can learn up to EIGHT MOVES at a time. Pokémon may learn any move in their natural learnset regardless of their Tier, and additional move slots can be purchased through the Pokémart.

Moves are not restricted to how they function in the video games. Pokémon are encouraged to use their moves creatively, provided their actions are logical and reasonable. Offensive moves may be used defensively, defensive moves may be used offensively, and multiple moves may be combined to produce different effects. However, using moves creatively does not automatically increase their power or allow Pokémon to perform actions beyond their capabilities.

Damage and Energy

A move's Base Power determines its general damage and energy requirements. Stellara uses the following damage categories:

Base PowerDamage
—Negligible
0–39Minor
40–79Moderate
80–119Heavy
120+Extreme
—Devastating

For example, Ember has a Base Power of 40, meaning it generally deals Moderate damage and requires a Moderate amount of energy to use.

Negligible and Devastating damage are generally reserved for circumstances involving type effectiveness, overwhelming differences in Pokémon Tiers or other exceptional situations.

Type effectiveness adjusts a move's damage as follows:

  • 2× Super Effective: Increases damage by one category.
  • 4× Super Effective: Increases damage by two categories.
  • ½× Not Very Effective: Decreases damage by one category.
  • ¼× Not Very Effective: Decreases damage by two categories.
  • Immune: The move deals no damage.

These categories are guidelines rather than guarantees. A Heavy attack does not automatically knock out its target, and a Minor attack can still be dangerous when used by a significantly stronger Pokémon. Pokémon Tiers, defensive capabilities and the circumstances of the battle must always be considered.

Stronger moves generally require more energy, and repeatedly using powerful moves will gradually exhaust a Pokémon. Combining multiple moves or performing several demanding actions in quick succession also increases energy consumption. Defensive moves require energy appropriate to the attacks being defended against.

Pokémon cannot fight indefinitely. Exhaustion must be portrayed reasonably, and healing injuries does not restore expended energy or eliminate fatigue.

Critical Hits

Critical hits occur when a Pokémon lands a particularly well-placed attack, exploits an opening or strikes a vulnerable area. They are handled narratively rather than through fixed cooldowns or random calculations.

Moves with increased critical-hit ratios, such as Night Slash and Psycho Cut, are particularly effective at finding openings. Moves that guarantee critical hits in the games, such as Frost Breath and Storm Throw, retain their intended properties when they successfully connect.

Critical hits may increase an attack's damage by one category, depending on the circumstances. However, players cannot automatically declare that their attacks critically hit or use critical hits to bypass reasonable defenses and overwhelming Pokémon Tier differences.

Moves and Abilities that specifically interact with critical hits retain their intended effects.

Status Moves and Conditions

Status moves generally require a Moderate amount of energy, although their actual energy consumption may vary depending on the move and circumstances. Defensive moves such as Protect are not guaranteed to completely negate incoming attacks, particularly when defending against substantially stronger opponents.

Status conditions retain their established effects but are interpreted through Stellara's narrative combat system rather than exact video game mechanics.

  • Burn: Causes ongoing pain and injury while impairing physical performance.
  • Poison: Gradually weakens and injures the affected Pokémon. Badly poisoned Pokémon suffer increasingly severe effects.
  • Paralysis: Impairs movement, reflexes and coordination. Severe cases may temporarily prevent actions.
  • Sleep: Renders the Pokémon unconscious until it awakens naturally, is disturbed or is awakened through an appropriate method.
  • Freeze: Restricts movement through ice formation. Pokémon may break free through sufficient effort, attacks or external assistance.
  • Confusion: Disorients the Pokémon, making its movements, judgment and attacks less reliable.
  • Infatuation: Distracts the Pokémon and makes it hesitant to attack the subject of its affection.
  • Flinch: Briefly interrupts an action or creates an opening for an opponent.

Status conditions generally last for approximately three turns, although their duration may vary depending on the move, Pokémon and circumstances. They cannot be maintained indefinitely without reasonable justification.

Moves that have a chance of inflicting a secondary status condition may do so when appropriate, but players cannot automatically inflict these conditions with every successful attack. Moves specifically intended to inflict a status condition, such as Thunder Wave or Will-O-Wisp, retain their intended effectiveness when they successfully connect.

Status conditions must be portrayed fairly and cannot be used to automatically determine another participant's actions or guarantee victory.

Stat-Boosting Moves

Stat-boosting moves temporarily enhance a Pokémon's physical or special capabilities. These boosts are categorized as Minor, Moderate or Heavy depending on the move's intended effectiveness and generally last for three turns.

Multiple stat-boosting moves may be used together, but their effects cannot be stacked indefinitely to create unreasonable advantages. Using several moves that enhance the same attribute does not automatically combine their benefits, and repeatedly using the same move will not continue increasing its effectiveness.

Stat boosts must remain appropriate to the Pokémon's Tier and cannot be used to overcome overwhelming differences in strength. Players are encouraged to use these moves creatively while remaining fair and reasonable toward other participants.

Accuracy and Special Effects

Moves that cannot miss in the video games are exceptionally difficult to evade, but they may still be blocked, intercepted or otherwise countered when appropriate.

Moves that improve accuracy make attacks more difficult to dodge without making them completely unavoidable.

Other move effects, including trapping, disabling, draining, healing and battlefield manipulation, retain their intended functions but must remain reasonable within Stellara's narrative combat system. No move automatically guarantees victory or removes an opponent's ability to respond.
