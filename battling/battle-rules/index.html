Pokémon battles are a major part of Stellara, and we want them to be as creative, engaging and enjoyable as possible. Rather than attempting to replicate the video games, Stellara uses a freeform battle system that allows players to approach combat in different ways.

While there are rules in place, fairness and reasonable judgment are expected from everyone involved. Winning is not everything, and players who consistently disregard these rules or cause problems for other participants may face staff intervention.

Freeform Combat

Pokémon can attack, defend, dodge and interact with their surroundings creatively. They are not required to use a move for every action. For example, a Pokémon may scratch, tackle or bite an opponent without knowing the corresponding moves.

However, ordinary physical actions cannot be used to replicate moves or abilities that a Pokémon does not possess. All actions must remain appropriate to the Pokémon's capabilities.

Multiple Actions

There is no fixed limit on how many moves or actions a Pokémon can perform in one turn. However, performing several actions in quick succession requires additional energy and becomes increasingly difficult.

Players must also allow their opponents reasonable opportunities to respond. Attempting multiple actions does not mean that every action automatically succeeds.

Dodging and Blocking

There is no numerical limit on how often Pokémon can dodge or block attacks. However, Pokémon cannot consistently avoid or negate every attack without reasonable justification.

Speed, positioning, injuries, exhaustion, Pokémon Tiers and the circumstances of the battle must all be considered. Even successfully defending against an attack may require considerable effort or leave a Pokémon vulnerable.

Players are expected to acknowledge the consequences of their opponents' attacks rather than attempting to avoid every disadvantage.

Injuries and Exhaustion

Pokémon accumulate injuries and exhaustion throughout battles. Both must be portrayed reasonably and can affect a Pokémon's ability to continue fighting.

A Pokémon may become exhausted without suffering serious injuries, just as an injured Pokémon may still have energy remaining. Repeatedly using powerful moves, performing demanding actions and sustaining attacks will gradually affect a Pokémon's performance.

Healing injuries does not replenish expended energy or eliminate exhaustion. Pokémon cannot continue fighting at their full capabilities indefinitely.

Pokémon Tiers

Pokémon Tiers represent meaningful differences in overall combat capability and must be respected during battles.

Lower-tier Pokémon may use strategy, type advantages, their surroundings and other techniques to challenge stronger opponents. However, these advantages do not automatically eliminate overwhelming differences in strength.

Moves, Abilities, stat boosts and creative tactics cannot be used to bypass Pokémon Tier limitations or justify unreasonable feats.

Fair Play and Godmodding

Players may describe their Pokémon's actions and attempted attacks, but they cannot automatically determine how another participant's Pokémon responds, what injuries it suffers or whether it is defeated.

Godmodding, powerplaying and repeatedly ignoring reasonable attacks or consequences are prohibited.

All participants must be given fair opportunities to react, defend themselves and contribute to the outcome of a battle. If disagreements cannot be resolved between participants, staff may intervene.

Battle Resolution

Battles generally end when a Pokémon is no longer reasonably capable of continuing, a participant withdraws or forfeits, or another appropriate narrative outcome occurs.

A Pokémon does not need to be completely unconscious to lose a battle. Severe injuries, exhaustion or an inability to continue fighting effectively may also result in defeat.

Players cannot unilaterally declare another participant's Pokémon defeated. Battle outcomes should reflect the actions, capabilities and circumstances of everyone involved.

Official League matches and other special encounters may have additional victory conditions or restrictions.

Item Usage

Items may be used during battles unless the rules of a particular event or competition prohibit them. League matches may restrict item usage depending on the circumstances.

Using an item requires the Trainer to act, which may expose them to danger during an ongoing battle. Healing items follow Stellara's established medical item rules and cannot be used to repeatedly undo injuries or eliminate exhaustion.

Damage Calculations

Damage calculators are not permitted. Stellara uses narrative damage categories, Pokémon Tiers and reasonable judgment rather than numerical damage calculations.

Players should consider the strength of attacks, type effectiveness, defensive capabilities and the circumstances of the battle when determining appropriate outcomes.

Fainting and Recovery

Pokémon may faint from accumulated injuries, exhaustion or overwhelming attacks. Fainting does not necessarily mean that a Pokémon has sustained serious or life-threatening injuries.

Serious injuries and potentially fatal consequences are generally reserved for particularly dangerous encounters, such as high-risk Missions and the Stellara Trials. Ordinary battles should not result in permanent injuries or death unless the participants have agreed to those stakes beforehand.

Pokémon require appropriate treatment and rest to recover from battles. Medical items may treat injuries, but they do not replenish expended energy or eliminate exhaustion. Severely injured or exhausted Pokémon may require additional recovery before they can reasonably return to battle.
